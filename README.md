  I designed this map with Vancouver bikers in mind. My map (hopefully) attempts to highlight ideal, and scenic, bike routes (hence the inclusion of the parks layer). The popups in the map displaying park names allows the map to serve as a guide for viewers who want to select which parks or community centres they want to visit. Other layers I could add that would make this “route identification” feature of the map more robust could be cafes, restaurants, bars, and so on. I think one layer of mine that helps this aspect of the map’s intent is the inclusion of contour lines. Even though I made them fairly faint, they are still very visible to the user’s eye. My justification for including these 2-metre contour lines on my map is that they can help map users select routes (e.g. many cyclists would probably prefer to avoid steep and/or long hills). 
	This part of my map (which I have chosen as what makes my map compelling excerpt) is a good example of what I just described above. The area in question (West Point Grey, just north of 4th Avenue) might be an area that cyclists looking at my map to select a bike route might not choose due to a) the lack of bike lanes or b) the steep hill evident by the large number of contour lines in close proximity. What makes this part of the map doubly interesting is that there are a lot of large parks in this region (Jericho Beach Park, Spanish Banks, Trimble, and Pacific Spirit which is not part of the data shown on the map but is still visible with limited knowledge of the area). The large parks make this attractive for a scenic ride but the contour lines and lack of bike lanes could be enough to dissuade cyclists from choosing to ride here. 
	As mentioned above, my map could be improved by adding more layers that represent amenities. It could also be improved by learning how to change the two popups (on the parks and community centres layer) so that they do not overlap, since currently, users have to close the front (community centre) popup via the ‘x’ in the corner to view the park name popup if they click on a community centre (blue circle). In creating the map, the easiest thing for me to do was to use the idea of ‘colour connotations’ for the parks layer (green). This allows users to be familiar with what the map is displaying. Conversely, I used yellow for bike lanes because yellow (in the context of this map) would not have an obvious connotation with another use, so I figured that it would suffice for bike routes. I avoided the use of ‘complimentary colours’ green and red because green/red colour blindness is a fairly common affliction. My use of the Positron “basemap” style from Open Free Maps was ideal in my vision for the map’s ‘visual hierarchy’ as I wanted my three data layers to “outshine” anything else. I also thought it was important to the usefulness of my map to include multiple (three, in this case) data layers. 
	One final note that I want to touch on is about my map’s features: the two popups and the legend. The popups I found were quite straightforward to produce; I followed the format from the Thursday 10/22 lecture pertaining to UBC trees.
